      What you are looking at

      The measurement

      The engine integrates the Sun, Mercury and whichever bodies are switched on, from the DE440 initial conditions at JD 2440400.5 TDB (1969-06-28). At each perihelion passage it records the longitude of Mercury's position projected on the Park et al. (2017) mean-orbit plane (ADR-0011). A least-squares fit of those longitudes against time gives the advance rate ϖ̇ in arcseconds per Julian century, with a jack-knife uncertainty. The thin wiggly line is the osculating ϖ, which swings by tens of arcseconds with the planets' synodic periods; it is drawn, not fitted.

      Short spans give wobbly rates: the periodic perturbations do not average out. Park's literature numbers use a 2000-year window; for a comparable figure run 1000 years or more.

      Planets (Newtonian pull)

      Each planet's gravity tugs Mercury's orbit around. Removing a planet from the integration is Park's “GM set to zero” (ADR-0014 P-remove). Venus (≈277 ″/cy), Jupiter (≈154) and the Earth–Moon barycentre (≈91) dominate; Saturn adds ≈7, Mars ≈2.5, Uranus and Neptune a fraction of an arcsecond. Together ≈532 ″/cy, the part Le Verrier could explain.

      General relativity

      The 1PN Schwarzschild field of the Sun adds 6πGM/(c²a(1−e²)) per orbit: ≈42.98 ″/cy, the famous anomaly. With PPN parameters it scales as (2+2γ−β)/3. The “EIH” option applies 1PN among all bodies instead of the Sun alone (IAS15 only). WHFast uses a GR potential that reproduces the same secular advance, only for β = γ = 1.

      Solar J2 and Lense–Thirring

      The Sun is slightly oblate (J2 ≈ 2.2×10⁻⁷), adding ≈0.03 ″/cy. Its spin drags inertial frames (Lense–Thirring), about −0.002 ″/cy. Both are tiny: you need hundreds of years before they rise above the uncertainty. The J2 and spin overrides scale the real physics and mark the run non-nominal; the display exaggeration only changes the drawing.

      Asteroids

      The first N numbered asteroids of DE440 (default 16) as point masses. Park finds ≈0.001 ″/cy for 343 of them, below what the viewer can resolve on spans under about 1000 years.

      Rung lab

      Mercury alone around a fixed Sun, to compare central-force models: Newton (0), special relativity in two gauges (SR-a 7.16, SR-b 50.14 or 35.82 ″/cy) and GR (42.98). The SR rungs show how much a “relativistic” label alone fixes; the split between SR and GR parts depends on the gauge and is not observable (ADR-0012).

      Why not 5600 ″/cy?
      • This simulation is inertial: ICRF axes, no rotating reference frame.
      • The observed ≈5600 ″/cy (Clemence 1947: 5599.74 ± 0.41 ″/cy) is measured against the equinox of date. It includes about 5025 ″/cy of general precession of the equinoxes (Clemence's value; the IAU 2006 pA = 5028.796195 ″/cy is marked UNVERIFIED in PHY-017). No inertial-frame simulation shows that part.
      • Totals depend on the reference plane: ≈575.31 ″/cy on the Park17 mean-orbit plane and ≈577.72 ″/cy on the fixed J2000 ecliptic (ADR-0011).
      • Adding 575.31 and 5028.80 does not reproduce the equinox-referred value, because the two numbers refer to different planes. The viewer makes no comparison with the observed value tighter than ±0.41 ″/cy.
